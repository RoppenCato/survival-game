# The start: what the good ones do, what ours does, and what to change

Written 2026-10-06 for Robin, from playing the first hour of our game against what Valheim's Meadows, Grounded's
backyard and Enshrouded's Springlands do in their first hour. The rule for all of it: a relaxing, slow game, like
the Meadows, where you take your time. The first goal stays **leave the island**, reached only after a **beginner
base** (a home, not a village).

## 1. What the three games do in the first hour

**Valheim, the Meadows.** You wake by a stone circle with nothing. Nothing at the spawn can kill you; the first
hour ends with a hut, a fire, cooked meat, a bow and leather, and that is the whole trick: a lot gets made fast,
every step pays out in a thing you can see. The world explains itself by being looked at: a ruined hut shows
how walls go up, a deer runs so you want a bow, boars glare so you make a club. Every new material pops a "new
recipe" line, so curiosity is rewarded in the inventory, not in a journal. The workbench wants a roof, so the first
house has a reason. Night is darker and louder than it is dangerous, so you go home, and home is warm: the
rested buff pulls you back to the fire. The boss stone at spawn gives a direction without ever being a quest.
Nothing drains you; food only adds. The raft is awful and freeing at once: leaving the first coast on it is a
memory. Sailing comes later, after the Meadows are learned.

**Grounded, the backyard.** Wonder from scale: familiar things made strange, so everything is a landmark you can
name (the oak, the juice box, the baseball) and steer by from across the yard. The analyzer teaches recipes from
things you bring it, so exploring and picking up is the way to learn, and the game says "peep this" rather than
"go here". Threats read from far (spiders look like spiders) and the safe starting ground is big enough to settle
in. The first base is a choice about resources that you make by looking. The yard is dense with small finds:
field stations, a dead ant hill, a dropped item, each a short story and a reward.

**Enshrouded, the Springlands.** You walk out of a vault into a view, and the view is the map: ruins, towers, a
glowing shrine, all visible, all places to go. The flame altar is claim, base and waypoint in one. Survivors are
rescued out in the world and brought home, and each one opens a craft, so the base grows because you explored
(this is our hird, almost exactly). Danger is a place, not a timer: the shroud is a zone you can see the edge of,
and the home side of it is calm. Ruins come with loot and half-built walls, so building is learnt by finishing
other people's houses.

**The shared lessons:** a safe, pretty start where nothing hurries you; things to look at that are also places to
go; a lot made in the first hour, each piece visible; learning by picking things up; a home that pulls you back at
dusk; the first boat as a memory; and people found in the world who make home better.

## 2. Our start today, from the wreck to the raft

1. You wake in a wrecked boat on a small island's beach. Three bare hints: walk over the things, press B, press Q.
2. Branches and stones round the wreck. Flint axe, knife and pick by hand, a stick sword and bow, arrows, torches.
3. Three lone trees near the wreck, rocks, bushes, berries with E. Boars charge, deer flee, snakes bite.
4. Build mode: walls, floors, door, window, a roof over a closed room, a hearth, a workbench (wants a roof), a rug,
   a bed, a chest. Comfort counts a room's roof, hearth, bench, bed, rug: at 4, Brokk will teach and the shipwright's
   bench may be built.
5. Food: berries, roast berries and meat, stew at the hearth; meals raise top health and stamina for minutes.
6. Leather drops from deer and boar; armour and the shield at the bench.
7. Night and dusk; sleep in a bed skips to morning; rested under a roof.
8. The broken steading a walk away: Hildir's burnt hall, a hut still whole with a bed, the well, a toppled claim
   stone, Brokk with his story. Six stone raise the stone; Brokk joins and can be given the woodpile or bench.
9. The shipwright's bench on sand by the water (Comfort 4). E steps into the Shipyard; logs, lashings, an oar; Finish.
10. The raft, paddled east to the big island: the hamlet, the cave, the troll, bears and wolves. "You have left the
    island."

It holds together. Played slowly it is an hour and a half to the raft. The pieces that already match the three games:
no hunger, recipes from the key ingredient, the roofed workbench, the rested buff, the ruin that shows how to build,
a survivor who joins, the raft as the first goal.

## 3. What is missing or weak

- **Nothing to look at from the beach.** The island is a beach, some trees and grass. Nothing on the horizon says
  "go there". The steading is found by walking into it, or by its chart mark. There is no landmark and no view.
- **Two homes.** The wreck's beach is where everything starts, so the beginner base goes up on the sand, while the
  steading, a ready-made home with a hut, a well and a bed, sits unused a walk away. The base is built in an empty
  field when there is a broken one asking to be fixed.
- **Too few small finds.** Between the wreck and the steading there is the land and its animals, and no story on it:
  no hunter's camp, no cairn, no fish weir, no dead man by a cold fire.
- **The first night is not a moment.** It gets dark and that is all. There is no reason to be home at dusk apart from
  the bed, and no sound or sight that makes home feel better than the field.
- **The first hour makes fewer visible things than Valheim's.** The chain is clear, but the hut is a box. Furniture
  exists and nothing points at it; the rug matters for comfort but nobody knows a rug is a thing until they see one.
- **Slow activities are missing.** There is nothing to do slowly on purpose: no sitting, no fishing, no watching.
  Gathering is the only verb, so the pace is set by chopping.
- **Leaving is a line of text.** The raft slides into the water and a banner says you have left. Nobody sees you go.

## 4. What the start should be like

A proposal, in play order. Each item says what it adds and roughly what it costs to build.

**Wake, and see where to go.** From the wreck, a thin column of smoke stands over the trees inland: Brokk's fire
at the steading. The wreck's mast stays as the beach landmark you come back to. On a low rise between them a
stone cairn, which when you reach it marks the big island on the chart and shows it as a hazy coast on the
horizon strip of the chart. *Cost: a smoke effect, a cairn prop, a chart mark. Small.*

**Walk to the smoke through small finds.** Three to five on the starter island, placed by the seed, each a short
story and a thing to take: a hunter's camp (a drying rack, a crate with arrows, a cold fire: shows what a rack and a
hearth are), a fish weir on the shore (fiber, and later fish), a dead man by a burnt-out fire with a flint knife and a
line of runes on a stone (the world is dangerous, told once), a hollow oak with a bee skep (honey, a dish), a heap of
cut logs someone never fetched (a woodpile: free wood and the sight of one). *Cost: props exist for most; a placer
like the steading's. Medium.*

**Make the steading the beginner base.** Hildir's hut is the first bed, its roof the first roof, and the burnt hall
is the house to fix: patch five gaps in the walls, roof it, put a hearth, a bench and a rug in it, and it is a home at
Comfort 4. Fixing is quicker than building from nothing and teaches the pieces by their gaps. The wreck's beach keeps
the shipwright's bench, since it is the sand nearest the steading, or the steading's own cove if the placer puts it by
the shore. Brokk's fire is the first hearth to cook at. *Cost: move the home camp to the steading, make the hall's gaps
match the first recipes, place the steading within sight of a beach. Medium.*

**The first night.** At dusk the light goes gold, then blue, and far off a wolf howls (there are none on the starter
island, so it is only a sound). Under a roof with a fire the room glows, the fire crackles and the rested ring fills.
Brokk says one line about the night the first time. *Cost: a howl, a crackle, a glow, one line. Small.*

**Two slow verbs.** Sitting: E on a bench seat, a chair or a log by a fire sits the hero down, the camera settles,
stamina returns faster and a short rested comes on. Fishing: a rod at the workbench (wood and fiber), cast from the
shore or the raft with a click, a tug after a while, a fish; roast fish is the third dish and the raft's food. Both are
things to do while the island does nothing, which is what the Meadows are for. *Cost: a sit pose, a rod item, a
cast-and-wait, a fish kind and dish. Medium.*

**The first hour's things.** Point the furniture at the player the way the rug is pointed: the hut has a stool and a
table, the hall's rubble gives a few planks, the Building board's first row is walls, door, roof, hearth, bench, bed,
rug, chest, in the order they are needed, and the rest below. *Cost: ordering and the steading's furniture. Small.*

**Leaving.** When the raft is finished Brokk walks to the shore and stands there as you paddle out, and says a line
when you come back. The big island's coast is visible from the raft before the chart says so. *Cost: one walk and two
lines. Small.*

## 5. The path, after these changes

1. Wake in the wreck. Smoke over the trees. The things on the sand, tools by hand.
2. The cairn on the rise: the far island marked. A find or two on the way.
3. The steading: Brokk, his story, the hut's bed. The first night under its roof.
4. Day two: the hall patched and roofed, hearth, bench, rug. Deer for leather, armour at the bench. Sit by the fire.
5. Day three: Comfort 4, Brokk teaches, the stone raised, Brokk at the bench. The shipwright's bench on the sand.
6. The Shipyard: logs, lashings, an oar. Finish. Brokk on the shore. The crossing, and the hamlet's roofs.

Three slow days, about an hour and a half, with the town only beginning on the far side: the claim stone and the
hird stay on the starter island as a beginning, and the real town is for the big island, as decided.

## 6. Order to build it in

1. Smoke, cairn, chart mark (the view).
2. The steading as home: move the camp, the hall's gaps, the hut's furniture, the board's first row.
3. The finds.
4. The first night.
5. Sitting, then fishing.
6. Brokk at the shore when you leave.
