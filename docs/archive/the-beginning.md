# The beginning: the child who survived the raid

**Robin, 2026-10-12:** *"Let's make the beginning more immersive. Maybe it should be the classic 'kid survives raid then works
for revenge'? Let's expand on that idea and what we can do."*

This is a proposal to choose from, not a decision. Robin's idea is the frame; everything under it is suggestion until picked.
`docs/archive/start-loop.md` is the older proposal for the first hour (the view from the beach, the steading as home, the finds); the
two fit together and this one answers the question that one left open: **why are you here?**

## 1. What the game already says

- You wake in a **wrecked boat** on a beach with nothing in your hands.
- A walk inland lies **Thorgunna's steading**, burnt: a hall with its walls knocked in, one hut whole, a well, a palisade.
- **Arnor**, the village smith, is the last of it. His telling: *"Two winters back a longship came up the sound: Ormr Midgardbane and
  his crew. They burnt the hall. Some of ours they took as thralls. Some went with them of their own will. The rest lie in the
  ground."* And when he has taught you the raft: *"They sailed east, Ormr's crew."*
- When the raft first touches the next island the banner reads **"You have left the island"** and under it, small:
  *"Day 3. Ormr sailed east."* So the game already ends its first chapter on his name.
- In the hamlet on the big island a villager says: *"Ormr's men came by last winter. We paid them in wool and they left."*
  and another: *"Pay, and they leave. Fight, and they burn."* He is still about, and near.
- A **dead Viking** lies behind the troll's lair on the big island with the **bronze arm ring** on him. Nobody says who he was.
- Further out: raiding, a hird of sworn fellows, a jarl in the south who holds the silver ring, and, undecided, **legendary
  Vikings whose names spread**.

So the raid, the raider and the direction he went are already in the game. What is missing is **you**. Today the hero is a
stranger who happens to wash up beside somebody else's tragedy.

## 2. The idea in one line

**The steading was your home. You were a child there the night Ormr came. Everything you do afterwards can be read as the
long way back to him**, and nothing in the game ever has to say so.

That last part matters. The rule here is that the game states no goals (no journal, no "avenge your father"). Revenge has to be
something the player *feels and chooses*, carried by places, by people speaking in their own voice, and by what you find.

## 3. Three ways to tell it

| | What the player meets | Why | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A. The place knows you** | No new scene. You still wake in the wreck. But at the steading Arnor stops his work and stares: he knows your face. The hut has a child's things in it (a carved horse, a wooden sword, notches on the door post where somebody measured a growing child). Arnor has dug graves behind the hall, and can say who lies in each. | Cheapest, and wholly in the game's own manner: found, never told. But the raid stays a story about the past. | **Small.** Lines for Arnor, four or five small finds, grave markers (the prop exists), a name on a stone. |
| **B. The memory at the hall** | You wake in the wreck as now. The first time you walk into the burnt hall the light changes, and you are small: **you play the last evening** as the child. The steading whole and alive, people at their work, someone sends you to the well with a pail. Then sails in the dusk, torches, doors broken, the hall alight. You cannot fight. You run, or are carried, to a boat. Then the ruin again, and Arnor behind you. | The strong version, and it keeps the opening as it is: you find out who you are **by exploring**. The ruin stops being scenery because you have seen it standing. Ormr is seen once, so that he can be known again. | **Large.** A child's model, a scripted evening, fire on roofs. See section 6. |
| **C. The prologue** | The same evening, but it is the first thing in the game: you start as the child, and "wake in the wreck" comes after. | The classic opening. Strongest pull at minute one. But it puts ten minutes of story in front of the first free step, and tells you what a found memory lets you discover. | **Large**, the same as B. |

**I would build A first and B after it.** A is a few days' work at most and nothing in it is wasted: every line and find of A
is still there when B exists. B over C because the game's whole way is that you learn by walking into things.

## 4. Who you are, and how you came to be in a wrecked boat

Two versions. They change Arnor's lines, how old the ruin is, and who you meet later.

**The child, grown (the classic).** You were small, eight or ten. Somebody got you into a boat that night. You grew up
somewhere else (fostered across the water, or a thrall yourself). **Ten or twelve winters** have gone by. You come home in a
small boat and the sea breaks it on your own beach.
- The ruin is old: saplings in the hall, moss on the well, the palisade fallen. Arnor has lived there alone for a decade,
  which says something about him.
- Arnor's line becomes "Twelve winters back". He knows you by your father's or your mother's face.
- Ormr is older now, and by now a great name: which is exactly what the legend system wants.

**The youth who was taken.** You were fourteen or fifteen. They took you as a thrall, and you have pulled an oar on Ormr's own
ship for **two winters**. In a storm off your own coast you went over the side with the ship's boat, or the ship itself struck.
You wake in the wreck.
- Nothing already written changes: "two winters back" stays, and "nothing in your hands" and the wreck explain themselves.
- You know Ormr's crew by face, and they know yours. You know which of your own people went with him willingly.
- The others taken with you are still at their oars somewhere: **the first hirdmen you might free are people you grew up with.**
- Less of a "kid", more of a "boy who came back".

**I lean to the first for feeling and the second for fit.** A middle way works too: taken at ten, twelve winters a thrall,
escaped. That keeps the child, the long years, the faces and the thralls. Arnor's line changes and nothing else.

**And whose child?** Thorgunna's own is the strongest: the steading is yours by right, so the claim stone, the hird and the kingdom
later are a birthright taken back rather than a stranger's ambition. A thrall's child or Arnor's fosterling would be humbler,
and makes the rise greater. This one is Robin's.

## 5. What "working for revenge" means in play

Not a quest line. Four threads that run through systems the game already has or has planned:

1. **Ormr is the first legendary Viking.** The undecided "names that spread" system becomes concrete: in every village
   somebody has heard something of Ormr Midgardbane, and what they have heard depends on where you are (a farmer in the big
   island's hamlet saw his sail two summers ago; a merchant in the south has traded with him; a priest prays about him). The
   trail is made of things people say in their own voice. East, where "rivers and trade" lie, is where it ends.
2. **The ones who went with him.** "Some went with them of their own will." Each is a person with a name, somewhere in the
   world, doing well or badly out of it. Meeting one is a scene: kill, spare, or take into your own hird. The first could be
   living in the hamlet on the big island under another name.
3. **The ones who were taken.** Thralls from Thorgunna's steading are scattered through the world's towns and halls. Freed in a
   raid, each can become a hirdman who knows you. This gives recruiting a reason and a face, and a raid a purpose besides
   silver.
4. **The ring.** The dead man behind the troll's lair could be one of your own: your father's brother, or the man who carried
   Thorgunna's ring away that night and got as far as the next island. Then the bronze ring is **your family's**, and taking it is
   not looting a corpse but an inheritance. The silver ring's jarl in the south could have been one of Ormr's captains, paid
   in land. Every rank of the ring is then a step along the same road.

**None of this is a gate.** You can ignore Ormr for a hundred hours and build a kingdom; the world just keeps mentioning him.
And there is a thing here worth keeping in view: the game lets you raid villages, burn halls and carry people off. **You can
become to somebody else's child what Ormr was to you.** The game need not comment on it. It only needs to let a villager's
child be standing there.

## 6. What the memory (B) would take to build

Most of it exists:

| Needed | Have | To build |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The steading whole and lived in | `Village.make` grows a living place from the same rules; the ruin and the living one can share a seed, so the hall you later mend stands where the hall you remember stood | The pair: one plan, two states |
| People at their work, who run and hide | Villagers with idles, jobs, fleeing indoors and hiding (the raid's own code) | Named ones: a mother or father, Arnor at the smithy |
| Raiders | The guards are already men in helm and mail who fight | Ormr himself: one figure made to be remembered (a shield, a cloak, a way of standing) |
| Night, fire, fear | The night lighting and the ambient sound of 2026-10-12 | **Fire on roofs and walls**: the one new effect |
| You, small | The hero's model and clips | **A child**: the same rig at child's proportions, the walk and run retargeted; perhaps ten clips |
| The evening as a sequence | Nothing | A small script of beats (a place, a line, a wait, an event), with the world's own rules running between them |
| In and out of it | The cave's fade | The change of light, and waking in the ruin |

A fair guess is two to three weeks of my work, the child's model and the fire being the slow parts. A is a few days.

## 7. What I would do first (A), concretely

1. **Arnor knows you.** His first lines change from telling a stranger a story to recognising somebody he thought dead. He
   says little: he is that kind of man.
2. **Things of yours in the hut**: three finds, each with one line when taken up, none with any use except the wooden sword
   (which is a club).
3. **The graves** behind the hall, and Arnor's line when you stand at them.
4. **A name for you.** Somebody has to say it once. (And so the hero needs one: see the questions.)
5. **The two threads planted**: one name of one who went with Ormr, one name of one who was taken. Nothing to do with them
   yet.
6. The steading as home, from `start-loop.md`: it is your father's hall you are mending.

## 8. Questions for Robin

1. **A, B or C**, or A now and B after?
2. **The child grown** (ten or twelve winters), **the youth taken** (two), or the middle way (taken young, a thrall for years)?
3. **Whose child**: Thorgunna's, or somebody humbler?
4. **Is the hero a set person with a name**, or does the player choose a name and one of the three faces (Eirik, Ásta,
   Hallvard)? A child in a memory has to be somebody.
5. **The ring:** may the dead man in the troll's cave be one of your own?
6. **The tone of it:** grim (your people dead in front of you) or kept at a distance (you are hurried away and see the fire from
   the water)? A child running from a burning hall is the heaviest thing in the game so far, in a game whose look is "happy,
   lush, green".
